A listing costs 1.25 list views and 1.55 detail views a month: one sweep at about 24 listings per request, detail fetches on the 4% that change daily, and first fetches for new inventory. Those two come out of the crawl design and are the same figures the price is built on. Agent pages are the one thing here nothing has measured yet, so the control above applies one flat figure per ZIP to every source. Real agent inventory will not be flat: Crexi carries commercial brokers rather than residential agents, and the rosters overlap heavily between the other five. The pilot replaces this button with a counted number.

On the clock: the batch volume above and the 12 second session mint are both from the model that prices this deck. Per-request latency through a residential proxy is not measured, which is why it is a button rather than a number. Cheap requests and browser mints run at the same time, so a pass takes the longer of the two, and it is the browser tier that decides at every setting. That is also why the server is sized on the browser peak rather than on request count.

Sheet 7 · What it costs, and whyClick a rung. Drag the coverage.

The bill is set by bytes on the wire.

One listing, three ways to fetch it · drawn to scale against 1,200 KB

45 KB of JSON on the wire, 75 KB metered once TLS setup and retries are counted. One 1.2 MB mint carries nine follow-ups near 150 KB, which averages to 255 KB. Zillow scores every request, so it never averages down. Rendered pages measured 0.97 to 1.45 MB in our own captures.
